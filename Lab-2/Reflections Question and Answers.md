# Lab 2 – Sprint Reflection

## 1. How did the estimated story points compare with the actual effort required?

Story points provided a relative estimate of the effort required for each user story. The 5-point stories represented more effort than the smaller 3-point stories. Tracking the stories throughout the sprint helped us understand the workload and monitor our progress.

## 2. How was the backlog prioritized?

The backlog was prioritized based on the importance of the functionality required for the Book Club Reading Challenge and Discussion Portal. Core features such as tracking reading progress, participating in chapter discussions, and protecting spoiler content were selected for the sprint.

## 3. How well did the selected stories align with the sprint goal?

The selected stories aligned with the sprint goal because they focused on important reading and discussion functionality of the portal. Completing these stories contributed to implementing the core features planned for the sprint.

## 4. What did the sprint reveal about the team's capacity?

The sprint showed that the team was able to complete the planned workload by dividing the work into manageable user stories and assigning story points. The burndown chart helped us track the remaining work and visualize progress throughout the sprint.
